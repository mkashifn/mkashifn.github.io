What is ISO/IEC 42001?

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is the first international standard specifying requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS). It is designed for any organization that provides or uses AI products or services, regardless of size or sector, and is auditable and certifiable by accredited bodies.

Risk-based

Requires AI risk assessments and AI system impact assessments over the full lifecycle.

Harmonized structure

Uses the ISO Annex SL high-level structure, so it integrates with ISO 27001 and 9001.

Trust & accountability

Addresses transparency, fairness, safety, security, and human oversight of AI.

Regulation-ready

Maps cleanly onto the EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF as an operational backbone.

Who it's for

  • → AI providers & developers
  • → Organizations deploying AI
  • → Public-sector & regulated industries
  • → Vendors managing AI supply chains
Certification cycle

Stage 1 (readiness) → Stage 2 (audit) → surveillance audits → 3-year recertification.

Annex A — 38 controls across 9 objectives

Annex A is the reference control set (like ISO 27001's Annex A). Organizations select applicable controls in a Statement of Applicability (SoA); Annex B gives implementation guidance.

A.2 — Policies related to AI
2 controls

Management direction and a documented AI policy, aligned with business objectives and reviewed at intervals.

A.3 — Internal organization
3 controls

AI roles & responsibilities, and reporting of concerns about the organization's AI systems.

A.4 — Resources for AI systems
6 controls

Documenting and managing data, tooling, compute, human, and system resources used by AI systems.

A.5 — Assessing impacts of AI systems
4 controls

Impact assessments covering individuals, groups, and society — including harms and benefits.

A.6 — AI system life cycle
7 controls

Responsible design & development: objectives, documentation, verification, validation, deployment & operation.

A.7 — Data for AI systems
6 controls

Data management, provenance, quality, and preparation for AI development and operation.

A.8 — Information for interested parties
4 controls

Transparency to users: system documentation, incident reporting, and information on intended use.

A.9 — Use of AI systems
3 controls

Responsible use: defined intended use, and processes for operating AI systems as designed.

A.10 — Third-party & customer relationships
3 controls

Allocating responsibilities across the AI supply chain and with suppliers, partners, and customers.

Plan – Do – Check – Act
